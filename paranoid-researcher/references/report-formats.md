# Report Formats

Answer first, evidence after. The reader must be able to verify the conclusion
without redoing the research: that means locations, versions, dates, and the
counter-case, not a wall of links.

Scale the format to the stakes. A two-line factual question gets the brief; a
decision with money attached gets the deep dive.

## Contents

1. Answer brief (default)
2. Deep dive
3. Fact-check
4. Contested question
5. Gap register
6. Research log / handoff

## 1. Answer brief

Default for "research this" when the answer fits on a screen.

```
Answer: [the conclusion, stated plainly, in one or two sentences]

Why it holds:
1. [load-bearing claim] — [primary source, version, date]
2. ...

What would change it:
[the condition, version bump, or new evidence that flips the answer]

Contested or thin:
[anything at `contested` or `secondary-only`, one line each]

Gaps:
[sub-questions you could not answer, and what would answer them]

Confidence: [high | medium | low | unknown] — because [one-line basis]
```

Rules: no methodology recap, no "I searched and found". If the reader wants the
journey, that is the research log.

## 2. Deep dive

For decisions with real cost, or questions with several sub-answers.

```
Answer: [conclusion up front]

## What is actually true
[per sub-question: the finding, then the primary evidence under it]

## The strongest case against
[steelman the opposing conclusion, then say why it loses — or concede]

## Where the sources disagree
| Question | Position A | Position B | Why unresolved |
|----------|-----------|-----------|----------------|

## Conditions and limits
[version, environment, sample, jurisdiction, date — where the answer stops being true]

## What would change this answer
[concrete observations, not "more research"]

## Gaps
[see gap register]

Confidence: [band] — carried by [weakest load-bearing claim]
```

Rules: every section earns its place; delete empty ones rather than filling with
"none found". A "none found" is only allowed when you looked and want that on the
record.

## 3. Fact-check

For "is this true?" on specific statements.

```
Overall: [true | false | mixture | unprovable as stated]

| # | Claim | Verdict | Evidence | Checked |
|---|-------|---------|----------|---------|
| 1 | ... | TRUE | [primary source, version] | read 2026-11-08 |
| 2 | ... | FALSE | [source says X] | read 2026-11-08 |
| 3 | ... | UNVERIFIABLE | [no primary found; secondary only] | — |

Missing context that changes the reading: [...]
Confidence: [band]
```

Rules: quote the claim exactly as written, because small wording changes the
verdict. `UNVERIFIABLE` is not `FALSE` — keep them distinct.

## 4. Contested question

When the honest answer is "credible people disagree".

```
Question: [restated precisely]

Status: genuinely contested — [one line on why it cannot be settled now]

Position A: [strongest form] — held by [who], evidence [primary, date]
Position B: [strongest form] — held by [who], evidence [primary, date]

Why the disagreement survives:
[different definitions | different measurements | different versions |
 different populations | missing data]

What would settle it: [the observation or study that ends it]

What you can act on now: [the part both sides agree on]
Confidence: [band] on the disagreement; [band] on the common ground
```

Do not manufacture a winner. Also do not hide behind "it's contested" when one
side is simply wrong — that is false balance, and it has its own section there.

## 5. Gap register

Ship this instead of papering over what you could not answer. Include one row per
unresolved sub-question.

```
| Gap | Why unresolved | What would close it | Impact on the answer |
|-----|----------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| ... | paywall / no primary source / data not public | [specific action] | blocks / narrows / cosmetic |
```

Rules: rank by impact. A gap that narrows the answer belongs in the answer, not
only at the bottom.

## 6. Research log / handoff

Only on request, or when handing to another agent. Not part of the deliverable.

```
Question, prior, and confidence at start
Rounds run, what each round changed
Ledger summary: counts by status, dead claims listed
Sources read, with locations and dates
Negative searches run
Bias audit blocks
Open questions
```

Rule: reference the ledger rather than duplicating it.

## Universal formatting rules

- The first line is the answer, never the process.
- Confidence attaches to the weakest load-bearing claim, not the strongest.
- Numbers carry units, versions, and dates.
- Every citation must be one you actually opened. A source in the list that you
  did not read is a fabricated citation with extra steps.
- Length is set by the number of load-bearing claims, not by how long the
  research took.
